The Quiet Current
Ines Alvor

A grid, like a good orchestra, owes its beauty to the instruments you barely notice. Written from a decade spent in substations, Alvor’s fictional history follows the engineers, night-shift operators, and small-town councils who kept the lights on, and asks what they would build if they could start again. Her argument is simple: resilience comes less from grand plans than from many small parts that can look after each other.
